DECISION 1The name
TOPOS is a proposal, nothing more. The five letters carry the level, the concept and both formats; topos is the place you orient yourself in, which is closer to what we mean than a map, and the or is the mathematical one — it does not exclude doing both, which is exactly what a Borel activity with cards does. Worth checking against the Erasmus+ Results Platform for collisions before we settle.

1The idea

Three teacher education institutions develop, trial and validate two activity formats for university mathematics — card sorting activities and game-based Borel activities — which let student teachers build mathematical orientation themselves, together with the design criteria that say what such an activity must do in order to produce orientation. Everything produced is published as an Open Educational Resource.

How must task activities in university mathematics courses be designed if student teachers are to build mathematical orientation themselves — and how far can AI support the design and the use of such activities?

We take probability as the single content domain for the first two years. It is where our Borel work sits, and it offers both real elevation above school mathematics and real content transfer into school statistics and data work.

We sit alongside the existing work, not against it. Interface tasks make the paths and links to school mathematics their explicit topic, and there is evidence that they help. Ours is a different route to the same end: students generate those paths themselves, out of the mathematical content, without the bridges being made the topic. Whether that builds orientation — and whether the two approaches then reinforce each other — is what we want to find out.

DECISION 2Do we open the assistants for reuse?
Three positions are possible: keep them as our own working tools; use them inside the project, with the research staff at all three universities, and test transferability there; or publish them for anyone to reuse. Our inclination is the middle one, taken cautiously — the team first. Keeping them in is cheaper, less risky and clearly separate from the Mercator project; opening them is the stronger impact story but turns a tool into a product we then have to support. The activities, the criteria and the instruments are the safe core either way.

One wave of data collection is an academic year, not a semester: Lucerne from September, Hamburg from October, Oslo from January.

Submission early March 2027 — the deadline has been 5 March, 12:00 Brussels time, in recent years — with the project starting 1 September 2027 and ending 31 August 2030.

Our three courses run at different times, and that works in our favour: Lucerne from September, Hamburg from October, Oslo from January (MAT4010, spring semester). One round of data collection is therefore an academic year rather than a semester. That gives us four months for translation before Oslo starts, and it makes three waves possible instead of two. The summer between them is free at all three institutions and carries the revisions.

DECISION 3Norwegian or English in Oslo?
If the sessions run in Norwegian, the analysis has to sit in Oslo. If in English, it can be shared between universities. Helmer's call — and it changes how the analysis hours are distributed.

The first wave is deliberately a baseline. The sets run as they are, which is what makes an early start possible at all — nothing has to be developed in the first weeks, only collected — and it gives the revisions something to be measured against.

We use the asymmetry of the two strands on purpose. The card sets are ready in substance and go into use in year 1; Borel has to be built first and goes into use in year 2, with its assistant developed alongside the activity rather than after it. A project that already collects data in its first year reads as feasible; one that develops everywhere at once reads as risky. The staggering also spreads the load, so that no single year carries both a major development effort and a major analysis peak.

DECISION 4Does an AI run during the Borel activity?
This decides whether we publish anything of the assistants at all. If the AI runs live, the assistant is the activity and has to be released with it, and users need their own AI access. If it works only upstream, Borel ships as a self-contained file like the sorting activities and nothing of the assistants leaves the consortium. The real development question of year 1: can what BorelGPT does at run time be baked in at design time?

What the prior work does and does not do. It gives us certainty that the activities work. It does not save us the work: everything we learn about orientation feeds back into checking, adapting and re-cutting the sets that already exist.

DECISION 8Where does the technical work sit?
Four to five PM, a separate profile, not part of a research post.

How much we actually collect, and why it fits. Each course has two or three tutorial groups of around five working groups, so roughly ten working groups per university per wave. Recording all of them on every activity would produce far more material than anyone can analyse. So the design separates breadth from depth: the capture tool records the card movements of every group on every activity — that is automatic and costs nothing to collect — while audio is recorded from four groups on two activities per university and wave, about six hours. Across three universities and three waves that is roughly 360 sorting processes and some 54 hours of audio.

Speech recognition runs locally, which keeps the recordings out of any cloud service and makes a first transcript nearly free. What no tool takes over is correcting the transcript where it matters and coding the reasoning against the criteria — and 54 hours is an amount two people can carry over three years. The sampling rule goes into the application, because the form asks for the expected number and profile of participants anyway.

What this buys, and what it costs. It brings the collection block down by about two person-months, which go back into the activities and the assistants. The price is evidentiary depth: a sample carries less weight than a census, and the question we most want to answer — whether orientation is drawn on when a decision has to be made — lives in the discussion, not in the card movements. Worth deciding together how large the sample has to be.

What does not get cheaper is managing the data: consent, anonymisation, storage and retrieval across three jurisdictions and three years. That now has its own line rather than hiding inside the collection.

5What is settled, and the frame

First, what is already settled: probability as the content domain for years 1 and 2; three collection waves, one per academic year, at all three universities — card sorting in all three, Borel in the last two; the transfer to further topics in year 3 for the card sorting activities — for Borel we do not promise it; the assistants are development tools rather than products; and everything produced is published as an OER, on a project site, in an archive with a DOI and on the Erasmus+ Results Platform.

Not to be decided yet: doctoral projects. The project must not depend on a qualification timetable — we plan work packages and person-months, and each institution arranges its posts as it can.
